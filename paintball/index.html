      Brain
      Start from

      Settings tunes the brain's 26 numbers. Neural net adds three small networks (where to move, when to push, how to move in a fight) that learn corrections on top of the brain you start from; they start out changing nothing and learn far more slowly than Settings, so train Settings first and leave Neural running for a long session. Standard bots and Rookie start training over with no champion, so you can show the bots learning from the beginning. Rookie is a reckless beginner who rushes into the open, never hides and ignores sounds and the minimap. Saved brains are kept.

      Each step nudges the brain's settings a little up and down at random, plays the two versions as teams of their own copies against each other and against standard bots and past champions, and moves toward whichever won. Winning scores, kills don't, and stalling counts as a loss. A new champion must beat the old one in a sequential test without making rounds drag.

      Training runs on background threads at full speed. The 3D view follows the current champion in its own showcase matches; the speed buttons control it.

              House rules

              • Names are shuffled and split into two teams at random.
              • Bots play themselves on a Dust II–style map: they path around walls, hold cover, peek, strafe and flank. One paintball hit eliminates a player.
              • Each marker holds 12 balls. Reloading takes a couple of seconds, so bots duck into cover to reload and push anyone they catch reloading.
              • Players duck behind crates, jump onto them, climb ladders to the raised platforms, and carry one smoke and one paint grenade each. Smoke blocks sight; a paint grenade eliminates anyone caught in the burst.
              • When the player you are watching gets hit, the camera jumps to the leaderboard leader.
              • Players spray their tag on a wall once a round, usually right after a kill.
              • With people playing, a match opens with a 30-second warm-up where hits don't count. Online matches are free-for-all: every player for themselves.
              • When a team is wiped out, the survivors are reshuffled into two new teams and play another round, until one player is left.
              • The last person on the field runs standup.
